Sun and planet surface maps
---------------------------
sun-2k.jpg, mercury-1k.jpg, venus-1k.jpg, earth-1k.jpg, mars-1k.jpg,
jupiter-1k.jpg, saturn-1k.jpg, saturn-ring-2k.png, uranus-1k.jpg,
neptune-1k.jpg

  Source: Solar System Scope (https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/)
  License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0)
  Attribution is displayed in the site footer.

  These are equirectangular maps derived from NASA imagery. Downsized from
  the published 2k release to 1024x512 - at the size a planet actually
  occupies on screen here, the extra resolution is payload with nothing to
  show for it. The Sun is kept at 2k because it fills roughly a third of
  the frame at the About waypoint, and the ring strip is kept at its
  published width because it is only 125px tall to begin with.

  venus-1k.jpg is the atmosphere map, not the radar surface map: Venus is
  wrapped in unbroken cloud, so the surface map shows something no observer
  has ever seen. The scene is meant to show what is actually there.


Pluto surface map
-----------------
pluto-1k.jpg

  Source: USGS Astrogeology / NASA New Horizons
    "Pluto New Horizons LORRI - MVIC Global Mosaic 300m" (July 2017)
    https://astrogeology.usgs.gov/search/map/pluto_new_horizons_lorri_mvic_global_mosaic_300m
  License: Public domain (USGS/NASA)

  Solar System Scope has no Pluto texture, so this comes from the USGS
  mosaic instead - real New Horizons data, and the most complete global map
  of Pluto that exists. USGS publishes it panchromatic (greyscale albedo),
  so HomeCosmos.astro multiplies it by Pluto's measured mean colour rather
  than shipping a colourised map that would imply per-region colour data
  this file does not contain. Bright terrain (Sputnik Planitia's nitrogen
  ice) and dark terrain (the tholin maculae) are real; the single tint
  across them is an approximation, and deliberately a visible one.
